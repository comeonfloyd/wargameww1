Deployment

  • Drag your dots to position them. Spend your 4 reserves with +Inf, +Cav and +Art.
  • Moving troops: select units (tap one, Box to drag a box round several, or All). Then either drag any selected unit to where it should go (the group keeps its formation), tap the map to send the group there, or press Line and draw a front: each unit takes the nearest spot on it. A cross marks each selected unit's destination.
  • Defence lines: select units, press Dig (D) and draw a line on the map: the selected units spread out along it and dig a trench that follows your line. Or tap a spot instead: they march there and dig in on arrival. Press Dig twice to dig in right where they stand. Trenches grow while the unit is still and out of combat (about half a minute for infantry; cavalry and artillery dig slowly). Troops in their own trenches take up to 45% less damage, and enemies crossing them are slowed. Shelling and fighting wreck trenches, and captured ones are only half as good. The longer the stretch each unit has to dig, the slower it goes; planned trench shows as a dashed line until work starts. You can draw defence lines during deployment too.
  • Ammunition and fatigue: the bars under a selected unit show ammunition (yellow) and freshness (blue). Fighting burns ammunition, which is restocked from depots (see Supply); out of ammunition, troops fight at a third of their strength and guns fall silent. Marching, fighting and digging tire troops, and tired troops move slower, hit softer and take more losses. They recover by resting, fastest well behind the line.
  • Fog of war: your own ground is always clear, but beyond the front you only see enemies within sight of your troops, depots and HQ (about 14 km; cavalry 20 km). Enemies that slip out of sight leave a faded ghost where they were last seen. Your guns only fire at enemies someone can see. Send a Recon plane (1 reserve, tap where it should fly, once the battle has started) to scout behind the lines: the strip it flies over stays visible for a while; enemy guns can shoot it down. Black flies recon too. Turn fog off in the ☰ menu.
  • Reinforcements: before the battle you may deploy anywhere on your ground. Once it starts, new divisions come up by rail: place them within 7 km of a major city (2+ points) or your HQ that you hold and supply; these are circled while you place. Black's reinforcements arrive the same way, so taking its cities slows them down.
  • Roads or Direct (button next to All, or R): with Roads your troops take roads and railways when that is quicker, even if it means a detour. With Direct they march straight across country, keeping their line, and only go round water.
  • Trench warfare: dug-in troops are hard to shift. Their machine guns cut down units advancing on them, trenches and wire soak up fire and slow attackers, troops assaulting tire quickly, and ground in contact changes hands slowly. Break a line by shelling it with artillery first, attacking with fresh troops, or going round it.
  • Supply map (button next to All, or S): colours your ground by supply. Dark green is in reach of a railhead depot, light green a cart-fed depot, yellow a weak depot, orange only a trickle, red cut off. Your working railways are blue and each depot's 16 km reach is ringed.
  • Railways (dark lines with sleepers): troops on a railway inside your own ground and out of contact travel by train, about twice as fast as on a road, and route along the lines on their own. Enemy ground cuts the line. Draw new lines with Rail (1 reserve per 12 km, built more slowly than a road) and new roads with Road.
  • Supply by sea: a port you hold that is supplied by land feeds every stretch of your shore on the same sea or lake, so islands, beachheads and cut-off coasts stay supplied as long as you keep a supplied port on that water. Away from depots this is only a trickle: build a depot at a port there (three-quarter rate) or on the beach (40%).
  • Ships: towns on a sea or lake shore are ports (⚓). Select troops, press Ship and tap a shore on the same water: they march to your nearest port, board and sail, then land, even on the enemy's coast (1 reserve per 3 divisions). Troops landing on ground that isn't yours arrive tired and short of ammunition, and guns that can see a transport will fire on it. Black lands troops behind your coast too. Try the East Prussia map.
  • Supply: troops within 16 km of one of your depots (stacks of shells) or your HQ restock fast; elsewhere ammunition only trickles in. A depot works at full rate only at a railhead (on your railway); fed by horse carts from a railway up to 25 km away it gives three quarters, by road with no railway near half, and in open country a quarter (marked ! or !!). Troops cut off from supply starve: they lose ammunition, strength and freshness fast. Build depots with +Depot (2 reserves, no limit); with +Depot pressed, tap one of your depots to dismantle it for 1 reserve back and lay field roads with Road (1 reserve per 25 km). Finished roads also speed up marches. Depots are lost if the enemy takes their ground, and enemy guns shell them.
  • Relief: drag a fresh unit onto a tired one. When it arrives it takes over the position and its trench, and the tired unit falls back to rest and resupply.
  • Breaking off a fight: troops in contact can't push straight through an enemy, but you can always order them back or sideways. Withdrawing units move faster than attackers.
  • Units take ground around them. Troops cut off from their own map edges and HQ wither. Water is impassable.
  • Drag a unit in a straight line and it plans its own route; drag in a curve and it follows the route you drew.
  • Orders route around water, rough ground and enemy troops. Troops marching on roads move 60% faster (cavalry 70%, artillery 80%), so long moves follow the road network. Forest, hills, mountains, marsh and cities slow troops (horses and guns most) and protect defenders; hills give the high ground. Hover over the map to see the ground.
  • Infantry is a box with a cross, cavalry a diamond and artillery a round gun with a dot; the dark part of each symbol shows its strength. Cavalry is fast: use it to raid, exploit gaps and run down cut-off troops, but it charges badly into trenches. Artillery is slow and weak up close; while standing still it shells the enemy up to 16 km away on its own, preferring troops already fighting yours, and smashes their trenches. Select a battery to see its range.
  • Fighting and shelling scar the ground: cratered land slows movement, gives a little cover, and ruined towns feed fewer reserves.
